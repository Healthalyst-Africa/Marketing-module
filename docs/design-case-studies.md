# Design case studies

Five health technology websites studied on 19 October 2026 as references for
the HealthAlyst multi-page website. Each was opened in a real browser at
1440 × 900; Abridge and Flatiron were also checked at 375 × 812. Type values
were read from the pages' computed styles. Everything below was observed on
the live sites, except where a limit is stated.

These are references for structure, craft and user experience. Do not copy
their copy, imagery, illustrations, brand assets, logos or claims.

## Why these five

| Site                                         | What it is                                                          | Why it is relevant to HealthAlyst                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Abridge](https://www.abridge.com/)          | Clinical AI platform sold to health systems (US)                    | Best-in-class visual craft for a B2B health product; how to make proof feel human |
| [Flatiron Health](https://flatiron.com/)     | Oncology data and clinical software for providers and life sciences | Audience-first navigation and the clearest contact and company pages of the set   |
| [Commure](https://www.commure.com/)          | Suite of operational products for health systems                    | Presents many products as one platform, like our six product lines                |
| [Helium Health](https://heliumhealth.com/)   | Hospital software, financing and data for African providers         | Closest market analogue: African institutions, several product families           |
| [Hinge Health](https://www.hingehealth.com/) | Digital musculoskeletal care for members, employers and plans       | Serves two audiences at once; outcomes backed by cited references                 |

Doctolib's corporate site was also opened and set aside: its layout and
typography were less refined than the five above.

## 1. Abridge

**Positioning.** "For every moment of care": one platform for health
systems, payers and life sciences, "built by clinicians, for clinicians".

**Navigation.** Four top items (Platform, AI, Resources, About) plus "Log in"
(outlined) and "Contact Us" (solid accent). The footer is a full sitemap in
four columns: Product, AI, About (About us, Vision, Press, Life at Abridge,
Events, Careers, Early career programme, Contact), Resources.

**Hero.** A full-bleed, candid photograph of a patient mid-conversation in a
clinic, warm natural light, darkened at the bottom left where the heading
sits. A small floating card at bottom right announces a product launch and
links to it.

**Typography.** Custom geometric sans (Avantt). Heading 1 is 72 px, weight
500, line height 0.95, tracking about −0.04em. Section headings are 40 px,
weight 400, set in two tones (a dark first line and a grey second line).

**Colour.** White page, near-black text (`#242220`), warm off-white panels and
one vermilion accent used for the primary button, small labels and a circular
progress graphic.

**Assets.** Real-feeling photography of care moments (hands, conversations,
corridors), customer health-system names, outcome figures, award and press
tiles (TIME, Forbes, Fast Company), a video testimonial and a news carousel.
No generic icons.

**Page flow.** Hero → scale figure with four named customers linking to their
press stories → outcomes with a single animated graphic → a three-step care
workflow (before, during, after the visit) → science and research → awards →
testimonial video → news → footer.

**Inner pages.** About opens with a large all-caps statement beside a
geometric photo collage, then a founder quotation, then a leadership grid.
Careers links to an external applicant tracking system. The contact page
redirected to the homepage from this location, so its form was not seen.

**Borrow.** Candid care photography as the hero; proof tied to named,
linkable sources; a genuine workflow sequence; a footer that works as a
sitemap; restraint with one accent colour.

**Avoid.** All-caps display headings; scroll-triggered reveals that leave blank
space while content animates in.

## 2. Flatiron Health

**Positioning.** "Oncology, answered." Data and software for life sciences,
community practices and health systems.

**Navigation.** Audience first: "For Life Sciences", "For Healthcare
Providers", then Company, Resources, Publications, search and a solid "Contact
us" button. Dropdowns are mega menus where every link has a one-line
description, and the Company menu also lists international subsidiaries.

**Hero.** A deep indigo field with a diagonal light band. Heading 1 is set
enormous and ultra-light (Helvetica Now, 170 px, weight 100). Under a short
bold summary, three buttons send each audience to its own path: For Life
Sciences, For Community Practices, For Health Systems.

**Typography.** Helvetica Now throughout. Body 20 px with 1.5 line height.
Section headings 40 px, weight 500.

**Colour.** Indigo and violet brand fields, white surfaces, black text, one
violet button colour.

**Assets.** A dot map of where their data comes from (their own data as the
illustration), a list of real publications with journal and date, news
cards with topic tags, candid office and team photography, leadership
headshots on a consistent light-grey background, and two security
certification badges (one is ISO 27001) in the footer.

**Inner pages.**

- About: brand-colour header band with the page heading; "What we do" (text
  beside a team photo, with a small section label above a hairline rule);
  "Our story" (founding year and parent-company affiliation); leadership in
  tabs (Executive team, Medical leadership, Meet our experts); latest news.
- Contact: heading "Contact us". On the left, a short form that first asks
  "I work at a…" and the reason for the enquiry, then name, work email, job
  title, company and message. On the right, direct routes for product support
  (email and phone), legal and privacy, press, employment and general
  enquiries, followed by office addresses for each country.

**Borrow.** Navigation by audience; mega menus with descriptions; the contact
page as a routing hub with direct addresses for support, press and careers;
leadership tabs; evidence shown as lists of real items with dates;
certifications in the footer.

**Avoid.** Ultra-light hero type at small sizes (it only works very large);
heavy gradient fields on every page.

## 3. Commure

**Positioning.** "The agentic platform for every voice, note and claim":
one platform of products for health-system operations.

**Navigation.** A centred pill containing Solutions, Resources, Company and
Careers, with a separate "Contact" button. Solutions lists nine products.
Resources are grouped by role (for clinicians, executives and finance).
Company lists About, Customers, Trust centre, Clinical council, News and
media, Events, Partners and Careers. An announcement bar above the header
carries the latest company news.

**Hero.** Black page, light extended sans heading (GT America Extended,
48 px, weight 300, tight tracking), a row of four proof pills (appointments,
claims value, health systems, sites of care) and a rotating set of real
photographs and product screens framed inside a glowing arc.

**Colour.** Black and near-black surfaces, pale cyan accent, grey body text.

**Assets.** Product screens shown on laptops in real settings, customer
building photographs inside case-study cards, quantified outcomes on each
story ("350+ hours saved"), a resource carousel.

**Page flow.** Hero with proof → platform explained as stages of one workflow
(front end, mid cycle, back end) → scale figures → customer stories → latest
content → a contact form embedded at the bottom of the homepage.

**Borrow.** Presenting several products as one platform organised by the
stage of work they support; a trust centre page; resources grouped by role;
a short contact form at the end of long pages.

**Avoid.** A dark, AI-start-up look; it would not suit an institutional
healthcare buyer in our markets.

## 4. Helium Health

**Positioning.** Digital tools and financing for healthcare providers and
decision makers across Africa.

**Navigation.** Products (mega menu grouped into families: provider
digitisation suite, financial solutions, public health and data
partnerships), About, Blog, Careers and an outlined "Contact Us" button. The
footer repeats the product families and lists many policy pages (terms,
privacy, information security policy, service levels).

**Hero.** Centred all-caps heading on a blue-violet gradient with a dotted
globe, one button.

**Typography.** TT Hoves. Heading 1 68 px, weight 600, upper case; section
headings 58 px with tight tracking.

**Assets.** Photography of their own field staff in branded shirts using the
software inside a real facility; a strip of funder and partner logos;
statistics per product family; a live-chat widget and an accessibility
widget.

**Inner pages.**

- About: navy header ("We're on a journey to digitize healthcare across
  Africa"), headline figures separated by vertical rules, mission statement,
  values, leadership, investors and partners, media coverage, contact.
- Contact: centred heading, then phone number and email inline with a
  response-time promise, then a two-column form with visible labels marked
  "(Required)", including a location field.

**Borrow.** Grouping products into families by who uses them; real
photographs of the company's own people at work in African facilities;
partners and investors on the About page; phone and email visible on the
contact page.

**Avoid.** All-caps headings, an unqualified "#1" claim, generic gradient
globes, and numbering cards (01, 02, 03) that are not a sequence.

## 5. Hinge Health

**Positioning.** "Pain relief for everyone", sold to employers and health
plans and used by their members.

**Navigation.** A utility bar (Log in, Request demo, Support, language) above
the main navigation, which is split by audience (For individuals, Benefits
leaders and health plans) plus Our approach, Learning centre, About and an
"Enroll now" button.

**Hero.** Full-bleed video of a real member exercising at home, white heading
(Brown, 56 px, weight 700) and two calls to action, one per audience: "Am I
eligible?" and "Request a business demo".

**Colour.** Deep green (`#00491F`) for headings and bands, a light lime accent,
white and cream surfaces.

**Assets.** Real member photography with the app's interface overlaid,
outcome figures on a deep-green band with footnote markers, and a
"References" list citing the studies behind each figure.

**Page flow.** Hero → product overview carousel → value for organisations →
outcomes with citations → closing call to action with both audiences' buttons
→ references → footer with organisation types, company pages, support
contacts and compliance links.

**Borrow.** Two clear audiences with a call to action each; every outcome
figure linked to a cited source; a closing band repeating the main actions;
support contacts in the footer.

**Avoid.** Consumer-style enrolment language; it does not fit institutional
buyers.

## What the five have in common

1. **One confident sans-serif typeface**, set large (56 to 170 px) with tight
   tracking for headings and 16 to 20 px for body text. None uses a serif.
2. **One dominant brand colour used in large fields** (indigo, deep green,
   black, navy) against white working surfaces, with a single accent for the
   main button.
3. **Real photography of care or of the company's own people**, candid rather
   than posed, often full-bleed. Product interfaces appear in context on a
   real screen in a real setting.
4. **Specific, sourced proof**: named customers, dated publications, cited
   outcome studies, awards, security certifications. Nothing vague.
5. **Navigation by audience or by product family**, with mega menus that
   describe each destination in one line.
6. **A persistent primary action in the header** ("Contact us", "Request
   demo") in the accent colour, and the same action repeated in a closing
   band.
7. **A company section with a predictable set of pages**: About, leadership,
   careers, press or newsroom, partners, trust or security, contact.
8. **Contact pages that route**: a short form plus direct addresses for
   support, press, careers and legal, and office locations.
9. **Rich footers** that work as a sitemap, with policy, security and
   certification links.

## What this means for HealthAlyst

| Area         | Change                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Typography   | Inter only, as decided. Display headings 56 to 72 px, weight 500 to 600, tracking −0.03em. Body 17 to 18 px.                                                                                                                              |
| Colour       | Forest green as a large brand field (page headers, proof bands, closing band) with white working surfaces. Cream only as an occasional alternate band. One accent for the primary button.                                                 |
| Navigation   | "Solutions" mega menu by institution type (hospitals and clinics, laboratories, pharmacies, dental practices, imaging centres, suppliers), each with its product and a one-line description; "Company" menu; a solid "Contact us" button. |
| Page headers | Inner pages open with a forest-green header band or a full-bleed photograph, a large heading and a short summary, instead of a plain cream header.                                                                                        |
| Contact page | Rework along Flatiron's lines: ask "I represent a…" first, then the rest of the form; beside it, direct routes for partnerships, investors, support, press and careers, plus office address, phone and email.                             |
| About page   | Mission, what we do, our story, leadership (tabs if there are several groups), partners and investors (real only), latest news, closing call to action.                                                                                   |
| Our approach | A genuine sequence, like Abridge's care workflow: the four existing implementation stages shown as a timeline with what the institution gets at each stage.                                                                               |
| Proof        | Use only what can be sourced. Flag what is missing (customers, pilots, certifications, leadership, figures) for the company to supply rather than inventing it.                                                                           |
| Photography  | Real photographs of the HealthAlyst team and of facilities using the products are the target. Until then, candid licensed photographs of African clinical settings, never posed portraits of models smiling at the camera.                |
| Footer       | Full sitemap, policy links, contact details and, once held, certifications.                                                                                                                                                               |

## Content the company needs to supply

The references show that professional health sites depend on real material.
The website cannot reach that standard with design alone. Needed:

- Office address, general email and phone number, and a support contact.
- Leadership names, roles and consistent headshots.
- Photographs of the team and, with permission, of facilities using the
  products.
- Partners, pilots or customers who have agreed to be named.
- Any security or data-protection certifications actually held.
- Press coverage, announcements and media contact.
- Careers information and the recruitment destination.
- Investor relations contact and any approved company figures.

## Interface and interaction details

Measured on the five homepages (and Hinge Health's support page for
accordions) on 19 October 2026 with the browser's computed styles, the
loaded image elements and the live carousel and video objects.

### Images

| Site     | Formats and loading                                       | Corner radius on photos | How photos are used                                                                                                             |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Abridge  | AVIF with `srcset`; 18 of 26 below-the-fold images lazy   | 8, 12 and 20 px         | Full-bleed candid hero with a dark gradient only where text sits; small thumbnails inside cards; geometric photo crops on About |
| Flatiron | PNG, all lazy                                             | 3 px                    | Team and office photos beside text in a two-column split; a data map as the hero illustration                                   |
| Commure  | AVIF, PNG and WebP; `srcset` on most                      | 10 px                   | Product screens on laptops in real rooms; customer building photos inside story cards                                           |
| Helium   | JPG and SVG; nothing lazy; every image has empty alt text | 0 px                    | Own staff using the software in a real facility; partner logos                                                                  |
| Hinge    | PNG with `srcset`                                         | 16 px                   | Member photos with real app screens laid over them; full-bleed hero video                                                       |

- Photographs are cropped to a few consistent shapes: wide hero (about 16:9 or
  wider), 3:2 landscape beside text, 4:5 portrait for people.
- Text never sits on a busy photo without a gradient or solid panel behind it.
- Radius is small and consistent within a site (3 to 20 px). None uses very
  round photo corners.
- Helium gives every image an empty alternative, including meaningful
  photographs; that is an accessibility failure, not a pattern to copy.

### Carousels

- Used only for collections a visitor browses: news, customer stories,
  product features, customer logos. Never for the main message.
- Abridge: manual only, with buttons labelled "Previous slide" and "Next
  slide"; the next card peeks in from the edge to show there is more.
- Commure (Swiper): advances every 4 seconds on its own, does not pause on
  hover, has no pause button and no keyboard support, although it has
  "Go to slide" dots with labels.
- Hinge: manual arrows, but the arrow buttons have no accessible name.
- Logo strips: Abridge and Commure both run a continuously moving strip of
  customer logos with no way to pause it.

### Accordions

- None of the five uses an accordion on the homepage.
- Hinge Health's help centre uses them for questions: each question is a
  `button` with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`, a chevron on the right,
  hairline dividers, questions grouped by audience, a region switch above
  them, and a "Contact us via phone, email or live chat" link before the list.
- Accordions hold secondary detail (questions, policies). Core content is never
  hidden inside them.

### Motion

| Site     | Libraries                                       | Ongoing animation                         | Reduced-motion rules in CSS |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Abridge  | Webflow, GSAP with ScrollTrigger                | Customer-logo strip (about 35 px/second)  | None found                  |
| Flatiron | None detected                                   | None                                      | None needed                 |
| Commure  | Webflow, GSAP with ScrollTrigger, Lenis, Swiper | Logo strip, glowing arc, count-up figures | 2                           |
| Helium   | WordPress                                       | One "breathing" chat button               | None found                  |
| Hinge    | Custom (styled components)                      | Looping hero video with no pause control  | None found                  |

- Flatiron, the most institutional of the five, has no animation at all and
  still looks the most authoritative.
- Abridge's scroll-triggered reveals left large blank areas in screenshots
  taken while scrolling: content is hidden until the animation runs.
- Count-up numbers (Commure) start at "0M+" before animating, so a visitor or
  screen reader can meet a wrong figure.

### Micro-interactions

- **Transition timing.** Flatiron uses 150 ms with a standard ease
  (`cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)`), Abridge 200 to 250 ms, Commure 200 ms for
  movement and 400 ms for shadows, Helium 400 ms on everything (which feels
  slow).
- **Buttons.** Height 38 to 46 px. Abridge and Flatiron use a 4 px radius,
  Hinge, Commure and Helium a full pill. Hover changes the background colour;
  Commure lifts the button slightly. Labels are 15 to 16 px, weight 500 to
  700, except Commure's 12 px all-caps labels.
- **Cards.** Only for linked items. Abridge cards end with a small square
  arrow button; on Flatiron the pointer-resting card's title showed in the
  brand violet.
- **Header.** Abridge keeps a 60 px header fixed to the top while scrolling;
  Flatiron's header sits transparent over the indigo hero and turns white
  once the page scrolls; Hinge's utility bar scrolls away while the main bar
  stays.
- **Mega menus.** Flatiron's open on hover and with Enter, and Tab moves into
  the panel. Escape returns focus to the trigger, but `aria-expanded` stayed
  `true`, so assistive technology is told the menu is still open.
- **Announcements.** Commure and Hinge show a thin bar above the header for
  company news; Abridge places a small product-launch card inside the hero.
- **Consent.** Every site offers a clear decline option in its cookie banner.
